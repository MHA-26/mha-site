Research support

Helping good research happen safely

Research helps improve care and shape policy. Because most mental health research involves people who need extra protection, we support researchers from planning to publication.

One central review board

A single, independent board reviews research for all MHA institutions, so standards are the same everywhere.

Research liaison desks

Desks at Accra Psychiatric, Pantang and Ankaful hospitals confirm site feasibility, grant site permission and welcome approved researchers.

Clear forms and templates

Standard application forms, protocol guidance and consent templates designed for mental health research.

Orientation for researchers

Sessions for researchers and institutions on how to submit and what the board looks for.

Quick check

Does my study need review?

Answer four questions to see which type of review your study is likely to need. This is a guide only. The board, not the researcher, makes the final decision.

1. Will your study take place at an MHA hospital or service, or involve MHA service users, staff, records or samples?
2. Will it involve people living with mental health conditions, people who may not be able to consent for themselves, or other vulnerable groups (such as children or pregnant women)?
3. Does it involve an intervention, a clinical trial, biological samples or sensitive topics?
4. Will you only use anonymous or fully de-identified information?

Answer the questions to see a result.

Types of review

Three levels, matched to risk

Because most of our research involves people with mental health conditions, full-board review is the starting point. Lighter review is the exception and must be justified.

Most studies

Full-board review

For research that is more than minimal risk, including most studies with people living with mental health conditions. Reviewed at a monthly board meeting.

Decision letter within 10 working days of the meeting

Minimal risk

Expedited review

For minimal-risk studies, such as non-sensitive surveys or de-identified records, and minor amendments. Reviewed by experienced members without a full meeting.

Outcome within 3 weeks

Very limited

Exempt determination

For a narrow set of activities, such as anonymous surveys or analysis of public, de-identified data. You must still apply; the board decides.

Determination within 10 working days

The review process

From submission to closure

Timelines start when we receive a complete submission. Incomplete applications are not queued.

  1. 1

    Submit

    Send your application form, protocol, consent documents and fee.

    Acknowledged in 3 working days
  2. 2

    Screening

    The Secretariat checks your submission is complete and assigns a protocol ID.

    Within 5 working days
  3. 3

    Triage

    The Chair decides the level of review: full board, expedited or exempt.

  4. 4

    Review

    Reviewers assess the science, risks and benefits, consent and safeguards.

  5. 5

    Decision

    Approved, approved with conditions, deferred for major revisions, or not approved, with reasons in writing.

  6. 6

    Conduct & monitoring

    Your study runs as approved, with amendments, safety reports and yearly continuing review.

  7. 7

    Closure

    Submit a final report. Your file is archived securely.

Protecting participants

What the board looks for in mental health research

Capacity to consent

How, when and by whom a participant’s ability to consent will be assessed, and re-checked if it changes.

Representative consent

Where someone cannot consent, a legally acceptable representative may consent, in line with the Mental Health Act, 2012 (Act 846).

Assent and the right to say no

The participant’s own agreement is still sought, and any objection is respected.

Psychological and stigma risks

Even a questionnaire can cause distress. Plans must cover how distress is handled and how stigma is avoided.

Privacy and data protection

Personal data is kept confidential, coded or anonymised, and handled under the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843).

Information people understand

Plain-language information in local languages, with interpreters, witnesses and thumbprints for people who cannot read.

After approval

Your responsibilities as a researcher

Form B

Continuing review

Approval lasts up to 12 months. Send a progress report before it expires.

Form C

Amendments

Get approval before changing your protocol or consent documents, unless it is to remove an immediate hazard.

Form D

Serious adverse events

Report serious events and unexpected problems within 24 to 72 hours.

Form F

Protocol deviations

Report any departure from the approved protocol.

Form E

Closure report

Summarise your findings and how you will share them with participants and policymakers.

Use approved consent forms

Only the stamped, approved versions of consent documents may be used in the field.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Still have a question? Email the Secretariat at info@mha.gov.gh.

My study already has approval from another ethics committee. Do I need MHA-IRB approval?

For multi-site studies approved by another recognised Ghanaian committee, such as the GHS Ethics Review Committee or the Noguchi IRB, the board may carry out its own review or accept the external approval. Permission from the MHA site where you will collect data is always required.

I’m a student. What do I need?

Student research hosted by MHA institutions needs review. Include an introductory letter from your institution and evidence of departmental approval with your application.

Who decides the level of review?

The board decides, not the researcher. If there is any doubt, the more thorough level of review is used.

How long does approval last?

Up to 12 months, or less for higher-risk studies. You can renew it through continuing review.

Can I appeal a decision?

Yes. You can ask for reconsideration in writing within 30 days, setting out your reasons. You may not start the study while an appeal is under way.

I’m taking part in a study and have a concern. Who do I contact?

Every consent form gives contact details for the MHA-IRB Administrator. You can raise questions or complaints about a study or your rights at any time.
